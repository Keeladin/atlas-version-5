# Lessons Carried Forward from Atlas V3/V4

Atlas V5 is not a repudiation of earlier versions. V3 and V4 established useful technical truths and exposed architectural limits that should not be forgotten.

## Keep or preserve as concepts

- provider neutrality and replaceable models;
- local credential custody and authenticated service identity;
- MCP discovery and invocation experience;
- Google Workspace integration lessons;
- restart continuity and durable local state where continuity is useful;
- exact effect evidence and deterministic verification where appropriate;
- observable provider/tool failures rather than confident fabrication;
- useful Control concepts for providers, capabilities, credentials, usage, and diagnostics;
- Chat as the primary interaction surface;
- clear separation between technical capability and actual authority.

## Reshape substantially

Memory becomes transcript-led continuity plus indexed short-term memory and asynchronous durable-memory processing.

Workspace becomes the maintained field of action around current work rather than a formal Work object or workflow stage.

Schedules remain useful as passive persisted intent plus deterministic triggers rather than a major interaction model.

The old capability inventory becomes the enabled capability projection of the Environment Registry, with progressive disclosure of raw tools.

Control remains useful for configuration, observability, and engineering diagnosis rather than routine operation.

## Do not carry forward as governing architecture

The V4 obligation-led execution path, runtime-driven planner rounds, mandatory capability-step decomposition, Work/Sources/Cadence as central abstractions, source-enrollment model, and general confirmation policy do not constrain V5.

They may remain historically useful code in V4, but V5 starts from the accepted model-led architecture rather than migrating their semantics by default.
